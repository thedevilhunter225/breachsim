## 1. About this notice

BreachSim is a security-awareness platform. Organisations ("customers") use it to run authorized phishing simulations for their own staff, show short training after each simulation and measure how awareness changes over time. BreachSim is provided by [Company legal name], [Registered address] ("BreachSim", "we", "us").

This notice explains how personal data is processed in the BreachSim service. It is written for two groups of people:

- **Participants** — employees and other people whom a customer enrols in its awareness programme and who may receive simulated messages and training.
- **Operators** — people a customer authorizes to use the BreachSim console, such as administrators, campaign managers and auditors.

In the initial release, simulations are delivered by email and by QR code inside an email. SMS, voice-call and synthetic-media (cloned voice or video) simulations are not used.

## 2. Who is responsible for your data

The customer organisation decides why and how participant data and its operators' account data are used. It is the **controller**, and BreachSim processes that data on the customer's behalf as a **processor** under a [data processing agreement](/legal/dpa). If you are a participant, your employer (or the organisation that enrolled you) is your first point of contact: [Customer privacy contact].

BreachSim acts as a controller only for the limited processing it needs to operate and secure the service and to manage its customer relationships: [Describe controller activities, if any].

## 3. Personal data about participants

A customer adds participants by CSV import, by manual entry or by automatic provisioning (SCIM) from its own identity provider. Depending on the customer's configuration, BreachSim processes:

| Category | What it includes | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and contact | Full name, work email address, internal employee ID, directory identifier, optional phone number | Your organisation |
| Organisational details | Department, role or job title, status (active, inactive, on leave) | Your organisation |
| Approved context | Short context notes and a public-profile summary approved by your organisation, a context profile derived from them, suggested simulation themes and training preferences | Your organisation; derived by BreachSim |
| Participation status | Consent status (pending, consented, opted out, deletion requested), with a history of changes, their source and any note an operator adds | Your organisation; you, through the employee portal |
| Delivery records | Whether your organisation's mail system accepted, bounced or did not send a simulated message, with times and the provider's message reference | Your organisation's mail system |
| Simulation interactions | Whether you opened the simulation link or scanned the QR code, which option you chose on the training page (for example "Report suspicious", "Verify independently" or "I would provide details") and when | Recorded when you interact |
| Risk and training | A risk score with its history and the reasons for each change, department-level totals, recommended follow-up, training assigned to you, whether you completed it and the time spent | Derived by BreachSim |
| Simulation content | Simulated message text personalised with your name, your organisation's name or your department | Generated by BreachSim |

**What BreachSim does not collect.** Simulation and training pages never ask for, collect or store passwords, multi-factor authentication codes or payment details; they record only that an interaction happened and which option you chose. BreachSim does not record whether you open a simulated email — only following its link or scanning its QR code is recorded — and it does not store your IP address or device details with your simulation results. Technical logs are described in section 8.

## 4. Personal data about operators

- **Account details:** name, work email address, assigned roles and whether the account is active.
- **Sign-in data:** passwords are stored only as salted one-way hashes. Multi-factor authentication secrets are encrypted, and recovery codes are stored as one-way hashes. Operators who sign in through single sign-on are linked to the subject identifier issued by their organisation's identity provider (Microsoft Entra ID or Google).
- **Session records:** session identifier, expiry, last activity and sign-in method. The application does not store IP addresses with session records.
- **Activity records:** an audit log of actions such as approvals, launches, policy changes, exports and deletions, and an access log of views of sensitive records.
- **Invitations:** the invitee's name, role and email address (encrypted).

## 5. How the data is used

Personal data is used to:

- run simulations that the customer has authorized, approved and scheduled, and send them through the customer's own mail system;
- show participants the training page that follows a simulation and assign short follow-up training;
- calculate risk scores, trends and recommendations, and produce reports and evidence for the customer;
- apply the customer's safeguards, such as opt-outs, exclusions, verified domains, working hours and frequency limits;
- operate, secure and support the service, including authentication, abuse prevention and incident response; and
- comply with legal obligations.

BreachSim does not sell personal data, does not use it for advertising and does not use it to train AI models. The lawful basis for processing participant data is determined by the customer: [Lawful basis].

## 6. Risk scores, training and reporting

Risk scores are calculated from fixed weights: for example, following a simulated link raises a participant's score and reporting the message lowers it. After a risky interaction, BreachSim automatically assigns a short training module and records a suggested date for a follow-up simulation, which operators decide whether to run. BreachSim does not make decisions about anyone's employment; any such decision is made by people at the customer organisation. [State how the customer will and will not use simulation results.]

Analytics dashboards, campaign reports and evidence exports are **pseudonymous by default**: participants appear under a coded identifier, not their name. A customer can switch its workspace to named reporting; even then, only operators who hold the restricted "risk identity viewer" role see names and email addresses in those views and exports.

Operators who use the participant directory can see each participant's record, including their current risk score. Administrators, campaign managers and auditors can also open an individual participant report with risk history, recent interactions and training; each time such a report is opened, this is recorded in an access log.

## 7. AI-assisted simulation text

BreachSim uses Together AI to draft the narrative text of a simulation. The request contains placeholders such as `{{first_name}}`, `{{company_name}}`, `{{department}}` and `{{role_title}}` instead of real values, together with settings such as channel, theme and difficulty. Before an operator's theme or instructions are sent, BreachSim replaces the participant's name, the organisation's name, department and role, and any email addresses or phone numbers, with placeholders. Participant profiles, context notes, risk and training history and contact details are never sent. The returned text is validated and personalised inside BreachSim.

BreachSim requires zero data retention on its Together AI account, so that prompts and outputs are neither stored nor used for training by Together AI. [Confirm zero data retention is enabled on the production account and record the date it was confirmed.] If Together AI is unavailable, BreachSim uses a built-in generator that runs entirely inside the service.

## 8. Where data is held and who receives it

BreachSim is hosted on Microsoft Azure in [Primary Azure region], with a disaster-recovery copy of the database in [Secondary Azure region]. Requests reach the service through Azure Front Door, a global edge network, so connection data may pass through Microsoft edge locations outside these regions. Evidence exports are kept in geo-redundant storage that is replicated to [Evidence storage secondary region].

Simulated emails are sent from a mailbox in the customer's own Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace tenant, using access the customer grants. That provider processes the message, including your name and work email address, under the customer's own agreement with it.

The current subprocessors are listed on the [subprocessors page](/legal/subprocessors). Where personal data is transferred to another country, transfers are protected by [Transfer mechanism].

**Technical logs.** To deliver and protect the service, Azure Front Door, its web application firewall and other platform services record connection data such as IP address, browser user agent, requested address and time. These logs are kept for up to 90 days [confirm deployed retention]. BreachSim's own request log records the method, path (with simulation link tokens removed), status and timing, without IP addresses. Sign-in and public simulation endpoints also count requests per source in short-lived counters keyed by a one-way hash of the IP address; the counters expire within minutes.

## 9. How the data is protected

- Encryption in transit (HTTPS) and encryption at rest by the Azure services that hold the data [confirm key-management arrangements].
- Additional field-level encryption for participant names, email addresses, phone numbers, approved context notes and profiles, recipient addresses on delivery records, multi-factor secrets and integration credentials.
- Keyed one-way hashes instead of plain email addresses for suppression lists and pre-campaign exclusions.
- Separate roles for administrators, campaign managers, auditors and risk identity viewers; multi-factor authentication required for administrator accounts in production; revocable sessions with idle and maximum time-outs.
- Approval safeguards that are on by default: a scenario cannot be approved by the administrator who generated or last edited it, and a campaign needs approval from two different administrators.
- Unique simulation links that expire and only work on the landing domain chosen for the campaign.
- An append-only audit log in which each entry is cryptographically chained to the previous one, so alterations can be detected.
- Every record is scoped to a single customer organisation.

## 11. Deletion

When a customer administrator deletes a participant, BreachSim erases the participant's record, consent history, context profiles, delivery records and links, interaction events, simulation responses, training records and risk history, and removes the participant's name from simulation content generated from their profile. The audit log records only that a deletion took place and how many records were removed.

Some copies can outlive a deletion: evidence exports already written to write-once storage, database backups until they expire, and entries already written to the append-only audit log [describe the treatment of audit entries].

If your organisation removes you from its directory through automatic provisioning, BreachSim marks your record inactive and stops sending you simulations. Your data is erased when an administrator deletes the record.

## 12. Your choices and rights

- **Opt out.** After you respond to a simulation, the training page links to your employee portal ("Your training and privacy options"). There you can opt out of future simulations; opted-out participants are left out when a campaign is launched. The link identifies you without a password, so do not forward it. It stops working when the simulation link expires or is revoked; you can also contact [Customer privacy contact].
- **Ask for your data to be deleted.** From the same portal you can ask your organisation to delete your exercise data. The request is recorded, stops further simulations and is shown to your organisation's administrators, who carry out the deletion: [Response time].
- **Other rights.** Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct or delete your data, to restrict or object to its use, to data portability and to complain to a data protection authority. Because your organisation is the controller, send requests to [Customer privacy contact]. If BreachSim receives a request directly, we pass it to your organisation [and help it respond within the legal deadline].

## 13. Cookies and browser storage

The operator console uses two strictly necessary cookies: a session cookie (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict) and a token that protects against cross-site request forgery. Pages remember your colour theme in your browser's local storage, and the employee portal keeps your portal link in session storage for the current browser tab so that it does not stay in the address bar. BreachSim uses no analytics or advertising cookies and loads no third-party analytics, advertising or font services. Simulation and training pages do not set cookies; an interaction is linked to you through the unique link you received.
